# Conflicts of Interest and Appeals

## Material conflicts

A material conflict is an interest or relationship that a reasonable participant could expect to affect independent judgment. Examples include:

- creating, operating, sponsoring, selling, assessing, or competing with an affected agent or service;
- employment, governance, funding, family, contractual, litigation, or close organizational relationships;
- access to non-public information or a direct financial or reputational outcome;
- prior participation that would make a person reviewer and sole approver of the same work.

Ordinary technical preferences are not automatically conflicts, but relevant affiliations and incentives should be disclosed.

## Disclosure and recusal

Participants disclose material conflicts as early as practical in the issue, RFC, ADR, pull request, or confidential report. The responsible unconflicted maintainer records whether recusal, an additional reviewer, narrower scope, or another safeguard is required.

A conflicted person may provide facts and technical input when useful, but must not be the sole decision-maker. Agent creators, operators, sponsors, and assessors cannot grant final conformance or deployment approval to their own systems. Repository or organization ownership is not a bypass.

Sensitive details should be disclosed privately to `security@emptyjars.net`; the public record may state that a conflict was reviewed without revealing protected information.

## Appeals

A contributor or affected participant may appeal:

- a scope, review, RFC, ADR, moderation, access, or governance decision;
- failure to disclose or manage a material conflict;
- a claimed procedural error or significant new evidence.

Open a new issue labeled `type: decision` and link the decision being appealed. For sensitive conduct, security, privacy, safeguarding, or retaliation concerns, report privately to `security@emptyjars.net` with subject `Appeal: open-agent-trust-standard — brief summary`.

An appeal should identify the decision, requested remedy, procedural or evidentiary basis, relevant conflicts, and what may be published safely. Disagreement alone does not invalidate a decision.

## Appeal review

A different qualified and unconflicted maintainer leads the review. At least two unconflicted maintainers should review material governance, access, moderation, conformance, or security appeals when available. If the repository lacks sufficient independent expertise, the appeal remains open or is referred to an identified external specialist; it must not be resolved by pretending independence exists.

The outcome records whether the decision is affirmed, modified, remanded for new review, or withdrawn, with reasons and any dissent that is safe to publish. Urgent protective measures may remain in effect during review. Retaliation for a good-faith disclosure or appeal is prohibited.
